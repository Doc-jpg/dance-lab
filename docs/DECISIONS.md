# Decisions and lessons

## 2026-11-08, v0.2.0, the quick check (Deren: "she has to literally be reminded all the time to go pee and feed the dogs... ask her if she's used the restroom lately, if she's drank any water, if she's brushed her teeth today, if the dogs need fed... tell her to take a pause and go do those things and come back")

- A card at the very top of Today, above the daily questions: **Quick check before you start.** Each line is a question with **Yes** and **Not yet**. "Not yet" turns the line into "Go now, before you start. The app will be right here when you get back." with a **Done, I did it** button. When every line is a yes, the card collapses to one line ("✓ Quick check done. Bathroom, water, dogs: all good.").
- Which questions depends on the clock: bathroom and water always; teeth, "have Bear and Teddy had their breakfast", and "dressed and ready for the day" from 5 to 11; "is there something you are supposed to do first (homework, piano practice, a chore)?" from 12 to 20; dogs' dinner 16 to 21; teeth again from 19.
- A yes lasts the day for teeth, dogs and ready; two hours for the bathroom; ninety minutes for water. Then it asks again. `src/engine/quickcheck.ts`, stored as habit logs with `qc-` keys so they sync and back up like everything else.
- The parent week shows the dog and teeth answers per day (not the bathroom ones). The "What Dad can see" card says so. "Fed Bear and Teddy" is also one of her everyday habits.
- Nothing is blocked and nothing is scored: the Start button still works. The card just sits first, and says go.
- Lesson: a new hook added below an early `return` on the parent page crashed the parent view after a restore (React "fewer hooks than expected") and the error boundary then hid Today too. Hooks go above every return; the acceptance walk caught it.

## 2026-11-08, v0.1.1, written for a literal reader (Deren, after Vivi's first screen: "Please be abundantly clear for a very literal 9 year old. wtf is 'stage' supposed to mean? Eval EVERYTHING." Then: "I mean she's a literal person. She takes things very literally. And she is nine years old.")

- **Labels say what they are.** The three looks were Playful / Calm / Stage. Now they are "Purple and pink", "Plain and simple", "Dark with gold", each with two color dots and a one-line description. The hero line was still Eli's ("one young athlete"); now "Stretching, strength and balance for a dancer. A little bit every day."
- **One word per thing.** "Session" is gone from every screen; it is "workout" everywhere, matching the tab. "Check in" is gone: the button is "Answer the questions", the done state is "You answered today's questions", the edit is "Change my answers". "Sets" are "rounds". "Log" is "add". Energy and sleep show words ("Good"), not fractions ("4/5"). "Wiped out" and "Rough" became "None" and "Bad".
- **No figures of speech.** Swept the library, messages, badges, cues and engine reasons for idioms a literal reader would take at face value: "secret button", "runs on", "in disguise", "showing up", "step one", "push through", "wiped out", "no big deal", "happy heart", "a string pulls your head up". Comparisons that describe a shape ("round your back like a scared cat", "fill the belly like a balloon") stay, because they are explicit and they help.
- **Every move name says what you do.** "Dead bugs (lie on your back, arms and legs up)", "Stork balance (stand on one foot)", "Plank (hold your body straight like a board)", "Wall sit (sit against the wall with no chair)". Body parts in plain words with the grown-up word in brackets once: "the backs of your legs (those are your hamstrings)". Levels read "start here" / "a bit harder" instead of beginner / intermediate.
- **Doses in sentences.** "hold 30 seconds, each side, 2 rounds" or "8 to 12 times, 2 rounds"; timed moves that are not holds say "45 seconds", breathing says "2 minutes". The elapsed clock says "so far", not "total". Empty cards (no mistakes, no versions) are hidden instead of showing an empty heading.
- **Questions are questions.** "How much energy do you have?", "How did you sleep last night?", "Are your muscles sore or tired?", "Is anything wrong? Tap one only if it is true." Class length is "Short (less than 45 minutes) / Normal (about an hour) / Long (more than an hour and a half)".
- **Workout names say what you do, not how it feels.** Deren on "Easy day": "Like today was easy? Or take it easy? Or I want today to be easy? Or it was already easy so give me something hard?" Renamed: Easy workout → **Short gentle workout**; Easy day → **Just breathing**; Daily 5 → **Five-minute mini workout** (so the 5 cannot be read as five of something). Headlines: "Today: a short gentle workout" and "No workout today: just breathing, and tell a grown-up". The word "easy" now only appears where it means easy ("how hard was it? Easy").
- Method: a Playwright script dumped the text of every screen she can reach (setup, tour, Today, the questions, every move page, the player, stop, summary, progress, habits, journal, settings, help), read as she would read it, rewritten, dumped again. Worth repeating after any copy change.

## 2026-11-07, v0.1.0, first build (Deren: "gear it towards Vivi... mobility, stretching, strength, flexibility. Bodyweight stuff only. She's only nine and she's not in sports. She's in dance and piano and sewing, and so we're just trying to create flexibility and athleticism and movement.")

- **Fork, do not rebuild.** Athletic Lab's shape already fit (check-in, color, one session a day, player, parent mode, sync, backup). What a nine-year-old dancer needs is a different body of content and gentler rules, not a different app. Same Supabase project, own family code, so no server work.
- **Body weight only, in the type system.** `Load = 'body'`. No weights, no approvals, no "5 lb" anywhere, and the library test proves every exercise's `loads` is `['body']`. The parent's Exercises tab lost the approval chips and gained "Mark watched" and "Turn off" on every move.
- **Flexibility rules baked in, not advised.** Every stretch holds 15 to 30 seconds (45 for child's pose), warm-up first in every template, no bouncing, "never let anyone push you deeper" on every stretch's skip line (tested), no deep backbends, bridges-as-backbends, wheels or neck stretches in the library at all. Cobra is a small lift with hips down. Half split is explicitly not a split. The dance teacher takes her further in class; the app keeps the daily habit.
- **Three green days that take turns: Stretch and reach, Strong and steady, Happy feet.** Instead of Strength A / Shortstop / Strength B. Only Strong and steady counts as demanding (48 hours apart, weekly cap of two or three); stretch and feet days are gentle enough for any day. A strength turn that is not allowed falls back to whichever of stretch and feet was longer ago.
- **A dance class is a yellow day.** A normal class (45 to 90 minutes, medium) scores 4, which is yellow: the Easy workout, eight minutes, gentle stretches while she is still warm. Wednesday is dance, so Wednesday is the Easy workout. A recital, show or competition is orange (five-minute stretch) and keeps the legs easy the next day. "Lots of jumping" (trampoline, tumbling) blocks jumps today and tomorrow.
- **Something every day, 12 minutes or less.** Same rule as Eli's app, and sessions are shorter because nine-year-old attention is the real cap. Daily 5, Easy workout, Five-minute stretch and the Easy day (two minutes of balloon breathing on a red day) are all there.
- **No body talk, tested.** Nothing about weight, shape, calories, dieting or what a dancer should look like, in the messages, the habits page, the FAQ or the library, and the tests look for the words. The habit list says "Dancers eat; that is where the energy for class comes from."
- **Words for nine.** "Balloon breathing", "Stork balance", "Happy feet", "a good pull, never a pinch", "tell a grown-up" instead of "tell a parent", "she" throughout. Exercise names say what you do, not what it is called: "Squats (sit and stand)", "Push-ups on a bench or couch", "Point and flex", "Towel scrunches".
- **Dance vocabulary where it helps.** Relevé, tendu, arabesque, butterfly, straddle, pike: words she hears in class, each with the plain explanation next to it.
- **No outside videos.** Athletic Lab's YouTube demos were for baseball moves a kid has never seen. Stretches and simple strength moves are clear from the moving drawing, and the family-clip slot stays for a ten-second clip of Vivi doing it right. Dropping the videos also dropped the link checker and the keepalive workflow (Athletic Lab's keeps the shared project awake).
- **Own look.** Plum and rose (Playful), Calm, and Stage (dark with gold). Icon is the ballet shoes emoji on plum, as asked, rendered from Noto Color Emoji the same way Dad Lab's arm was, with the `-v4` cache-busting lesson not needed on a fresh install.
- **Setup speaks to the parent, then to her.** Activities list is dance, gymnastics, swimming, piano, sewing, other (piano and sewing are not activity logs; they are there so the profile reads true to her). Birth year accepts down to four years ago.

## Lessons for the first real week
- Watch which day she lands on after Wednesday dance; if the Easy workout feels too long at eight minutes for a tired kid, the fix is the template, not the rule.
- If she never picks Happy feet on her own, move it earlier in the rotation or fold relevés into the Daily 5.
- The parent "Mark watched" flag has no effect on the shipped library (no move needs a watch first). It is there for the day a harder move does.
